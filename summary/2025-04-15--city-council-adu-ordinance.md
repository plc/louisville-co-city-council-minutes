## Summary
Council advanced Ordinance 1892 allowing Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) citywide to comply with state law requiring implementation by June 2025. The ordinance permits ADUs up to 800 sq ft (detached) or 1,000 sq ft (attached), with 200 sq ft bonuses for affordable, accessible, or landmarked units. Tap fees remain a major cost barrier but are not addressed in this ordinance; staff indicated future discussion is planned.

## Key Points
- **Ordinance 1892 (second reading):** Allows ADUs citywide as required by state law (June deadline)
- **Size limits:** 800 sq ft detached, 1,000 sq ft attached (with 200 sq ft incentive bonuses)
- **Height:** 20 ft standard, 25 ft in Old Town (to accommodate roof pitch requirements)
- **Parking:** Rarely required (only when no on-street parking + no excess space on lot)
- **Grace period:** Existing illegal ADUs can legalize through end of 2026 (safety inspection required)
- **Impact fees:** First 500 sq ft exempt; tap fees not addressed (future discussion)
- **No STRs:** Short-term rentals remain prohibited in ADUs
- **Alternative proposal:** Architect group submitted detailed modifications (lot coverage, height, townhome ADUs)
- **Marshall Fire update:** 92% of destroyed homes in rebuild process, 76% using 2021+ building code
- **Via Appia safety:** Resident demanded immediate fix for dangerous Tyler/Via Appia intersection striping
- **Arbor Day:** Louisville named Tree City USA for 44th consecutive year
- **Chamber update:** Pints in the Park grew from 4 to 50 vendors, 500 to 800+ attendees

## Takeaways
The ADU ordinance represents Louisville catching up to peer cities while navigating state mandates that limit local control. Staff designed the ordinance to be minimally compliant with state law while offering modest incentives for affordable housing, accessibility, and historic preservation. The 200 sq ft bonuses and impact fee exemptions are carrots, but the tap fee elephant remains in the room—staff acknowledged it's a "significant cost barrier" without proposing solutions, kicking that discussion down the road.

The alternative proposal from architects suggests professionals see room for more permissive regulations, particularly around lot coverage and expanding ADUs to townhomes/duplexes (not required by state law). Staff's resistance centers on "neighborhood character" concerns, a traditional planning argument that the state law explicitly tried to override for single-family homes.

The Via Appia striping issue continues to escalate, with a resident reporting daily near-misses and a recent motorcycle incident within "two feet" of collision. The removed right-turn lane and single-lane configuration appear to be creating unpredictable driver behavior. Council made no immediate response.

Marshall Fire recovery data shows remarkable progress (92% rebuild rate) and voluntary adoption of stronger building codes (76% to 2021 standards), suggesting community commitment to resilience outweighs minimum requirements—a stark contrast to the ADU ordinance approach.
