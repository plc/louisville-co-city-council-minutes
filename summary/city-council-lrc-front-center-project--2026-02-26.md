## Summary

Louisville City Council held a special joint meeting with the Louisville Revitalization Commission (LRC) on February 25, 2026, to review three major items: a fourth amended cooperation agreement defining authority relationships, the Front and Center downtown revitalization project at 30% design completion, and potential new urban renewal areas. The meeting ran over three hours with full council and LRC attendance.

The cooperation agreement discussion clarified the division of authority between City Council and the LRC. Council retains final approval over urban renewal plans, TIF (Tax Increment Financing) agreements, and bond issuance, while the LRC gains more autonomy in detailed project design and programming once high-level plans are approved. This framework allows the LRC to execute projects more efficiently while maintaining democratic oversight on major decisions and financial commitments.

The Front and Center project dominated discussion, with council providing critical direction on a ~$765,000 downtown improvement initiative. The project focuses primarily on Front Street (creating a plaza/gathering space between the library and arts center) with secondary attention to Main Street. Initial designs sparked council concern that Main Street—"the main avenue of Louisville"—received minimal improvements while Front Street got the bulk of investment. Council members, particularly Mayor Pro Tem Hamilton and Council Member Kern, pushed for rebalancing priorities to include meaningful Main Street enhancements (lighting, plantings) even if it meant cutting elements like the splash pad or large event tent infrastructure from the Front Street design. Council emphasized budget discipline (prefer cost cuts before tapping contingency funds) but signaled willingness to provide additional funding if needed, delegating detailed programming decisions to the LRC within the approved framework.

Key tensions emerged around mission alignment: Council Member Kern questioned whether splash pads and library amenities truly serve the LRC's statutory blight elimination purpose, suggesting the focus should remain on Main Street visibility and urban renewal fundamentals. ADA compliance was repeatedly emphasized (accessible benches, tiered seating), along with practical concerns like food truck electrical infrastructure, bicycle parking, and coordination with existing community events (farmers market). The Chamber of Commerce expressed strong support for the project while emphasizing the importance of minimizing business disruption during construction.

## Key Points

- **Cooperation Agreement:** Fourth amendment clarifies authority split—council approves plans/TIF/bonds, LRC executes detailed design/programming
- **Budget:** ~$765K TIF funding for Front and Center project; council willing to provide contingency but prefers cost cuts first
- **Main Street vs. Front Street:** Council pushed back on Front Street-heavy design, demanding meaningful Main Street improvements (lighting, plantings)
- **Design elements debated:**
  - **Keep/prioritize:** Main Street lighting and plantings, ADA features, food truck electrical, bicycle parking
  - **Question/potential cuts:** Splash pad, large event tent, expensive pavers
- **Authority delegation:** LRC can make programming decisions within approved budget; new elements require council approval
- **Mission focus:** Emphasis on blight elimination (LRC's core purpose) vs. general amenity improvements
- **Business impacts:** Chamber of Commerce engaged; farmers market coordination important
- **ADA compliance:** Repeated emphasis on accessible benches, seating, pathways
- **New urban renewal areas:** No specific proposals; LRC will follow proper process when plans emerge

## Takeaways

This meeting represents a critical checkpoint in Louisville's downtown revitalization strategy, balancing the efficiency of delegated authority against democratic oversight and fiscal responsibility. The cooperation agreement framework appears to resolve longstanding tensions about who decides what, allowing the LRC to operate with more autonomy while ensuring council retains control over major financial and policy commitments. The Front and Center project reveals ongoing community debates about investment priorities (Front Street placemaking vs. Main Street visibility), spending discipline (cutting nice-to-haves before requesting more money), and mission creep (blight elimination vs. general beautification). Council's willingness to provide contingency funding—conditioned on demonstrated cost-cutting efforts—suggests strong support for downtown revitalization tempered by fiscal caution. The unanimous emphasis on ADA compliance, business impact mitigation, and Main Street enhancement indicates these values are non-negotiable across the political spectrum. Implementation will test whether the LRC can balance competing demands while staying within budget and timeline constraints.
