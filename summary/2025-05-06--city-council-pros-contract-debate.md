## Summary
Public comment dominated the May 6 meeting with former Boulder Open Space planner and environmental advocates urging council to pull the Parks, Recreation & Open Space (PROS) long-range plan contract from consent agenda, citing missing ecological inventory requirements and vague deliverables. Council also advanced Ordinance 1895 (contractor insurance requirements) on first reading with new presentation format, and celebrated Louisville's ranking as #1 city in Boulder County.

## Key Points
- **PROS contract criticism:** Katherine Smith (LOSA), Tamar Krauss, Matt Jones (ex-Boulder OSMP planner) requested contract removal from consent agenda
- **Key exclusions cited:** Open space/wildlife/habitat inventory, management plans, native prairie restoration, field ecological work
- **Missing specs:** No defined deliverables for open space work, subcontractor ERO role undefined, no DTJ-ERO requirement in contract
- **Open space funding:** Concerns about "historical pattern" of using open space as "slush fund" for other divisions
- **Property acquisition:** Request to pursue 96 Dylan property (bordered 3 sides by open space)
- **Via Appia underpass:** James Williams presented resolution with 50 signatures for South Boulder Road crossing
- **Ordinance 1895 (first reading):** Contractor insurance verification, 2018+ code testing, supervisor requirements, stop-work order authority
- **Process change:** First reading now includes full staff presentation (2-week buffer for public/council feedback)
- **Louisville ranked:** #1 city in Boulder County, #137 nationally (niche.com, 3,164 counties evaluated)
- **Monarch parade:** Louisville back in May 10 graduation parade (4-5pm)

## Takeaways
The PROS contract debate reveals a fundamental tension between Louisville's environmental stewardship charter obligations and staff planning processes. Matt Jones' 18-year Boulder OSMP experience lends credibility to concerns that the contract puts the cart before the horse—planning trails and facilities without first conducting ecological inventory. His warning that "it's going to be hard to overrule what's in [the master plan]" later suggests the exclusion of habitat data now forecloses science-based decisions downstream.

Katherine Smith's "slush fund" accusation points to budget allocation distrust—open space funding sourced from dedicated revenues being spent on non-open-space work. The vague ERO subcontractor language supports concerns that what was presented as ecological expertise integration lacks contractual teeth.

The new first-reading presentation format represents Louisville's attempt to avoid ADU-ordinance-style last-minute concerns, giving council and public two weeks to digest proposals before public hearings. Council Member Cooperman's heat-pump training request tests this new process—is first reading for questions only, or can substantive amendments be previewed?

Louisville's #1 Boulder County ranking validates quality-of-life investments but also raises stakes for PROS planning—the outdoor activities and environmental quality driving that ranking depend on getting open space management right. The consent agenda vote result (one "nay") and any subsequent action on the contract aren't clear from the excerpt, leaving the central drama unresolved.
