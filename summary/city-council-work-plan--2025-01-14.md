# Summary

Louisville City Council held a special meeting to review and refine the 2025 Work Plan, which identifies council priorities beyond day-to-day operations. The plan focuses on four strategic priorities (economic vitality, core services, safety, and affordable housing) with two cross-cutting lenses (equity/diversity/inclusion and environment). Public comment emphasized the need for comprehensive wildfire mitigation planning and emergency preparedness following the Los Angeles fires, as well as expanded nonprofit partnerships for affordable housing. Staff presented a revised strategic framework emphasizing resource constraints, the hierarchy of needs, and alignment between the work plan, budget, and comprehensive plan.

## Key Points

- **Four Strategic Priorities:** Economic vitality (comprehensive plan, downtown vision, business-friendly code); core services (infrastructure, public works); safety (wildfire mitigation, emergency preparedness); affordable housing (expedited review, partnerships)
- **Two Cross-Cutting Lenses:** Equity, diversity, and inclusion (EDI); environmental sustainability
- **Public Comment Focus:** Strong calls for citywide wildfire mitigation plan beyond the 2023 risk assessment (which only covered city-owned land), evacuation planning publication, and functional emergency preparedness partnerships with county disaster management
- **Process Changes:** Work plan moved before budget process to inform spending decisions; intentional alignment with comprehensive plan and community survey results
- **Resource Reality Check:** Interim City Manager acknowledged Louisville has "gotten a little bit good at doing more with less and sometimes that's not an achievable model"
- **Guiding Documents:** City charter, municipal code, 2025-26 budget, 2024 community survey, comprehensive plan (pending 2025 adoption)
- **Partners Identified:** Louisville Chamber, Downtown Business Association, Louisville Revitalization (economic vitality); nonprofit housing providers (affordable housing); county disaster management (safety)

## Takeaways

The 2025 Work Plan represents an evolution toward more structured, realistic council priority-setting. The emphasis on aligning strategic documents (work plan, budget, comprehensive plan, community survey) reflects a maturing governance process, though public comment revealed gaps—particularly around wildfire mitigation and emergency preparedness—that may require mid-year adjustments. Staff's acknowledgment of resource constraints and the "hierarchy of needs" signals a potential shift from Louisville's historically aspirational stance toward more disciplined service-level decision-making.

The public's wildfire concerns, amplified by the Los Angeles fires, highlight a disconnect: the city completed a 2023 risk assessment for city-owned infrastructure but hasn't published a comprehensive mitigation or evacuation plan covering residential and commercial properties. This gap—three years after the Marshall Fire—suggests the work plan's "safety" priority may need more specificity around community-wide disaster preparedness beyond the generic placeholder currently in the draft.
