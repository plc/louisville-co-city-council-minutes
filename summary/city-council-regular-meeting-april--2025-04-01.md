## Summary
The April 1st regular council meeting welcomed new City Manager Diane Langley and advanced utility rate adjustments for 2025. Council passed Ordinance 1894 (first reading) authorizing a loan from the Water Utility Fund to the Wastewater Utility Fund at 4.8% interest, with public hearing set for April 15. The water fund overperformed by $726,000 (10%) in 2024 due to dry weather.

## Key Points
- New City Manager Diane Langley officially welcomed
- Ordinance 1894: Water-to-Wastewater fund loan ($4.8% interest, 3-year term) - first reading approved
- Resolution 23: Utility rate adjustments proposed for 2025
- Water fund 10% over target in 2024 ($726k surplus) due to dry conditions
- Public comment raised climate concerns and block party permit timeline questions
- Mayor Leiker traveling to DC for transportation advocacy (Highway 42, rail) with new administration
- Buddy Bench recycling event Saturday (collecting #2 plastics)

## Takeaways
The meeting marked a leadership transition with Diane Langley's first official meeting as City Manager, though no major policy shifts were announced. The utility rate discussion highlighted Louisville's revenue volatility tied to weather patterns, with year-to-year swings of 30-40% as climate impacts intensify. Director Copher noted the 2024 water fund surplus helps recover from prior wet-year shortfalls but doesn't eliminate structural challenges. The DC trip represents Louisville's first engagement with the new federal administration on regional transportation priorities, particularly Highway 42 improvements and potential rail expansion along the Highway 36 corridor.

Public engagement included a resident's call for bolder climate action (including rewilding the golf course) and practical concerns about block party permitting requirements, which currently mandate 21-day advance applications without clearly defining when permits are required.
