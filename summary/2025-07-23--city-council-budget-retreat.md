## Summary
Annual budget retreat marked City Manager Diana Langley's first budget cycle, occurring during "busy year" with comprehensive plan and elections. Council reviewed fiscal priorities in second year of two-year budget cycle.

## Key Points
- Annual budget retreat (second year of two-year cycle)
- City Manager Langley's first budget retreat
- Context: "busy year" with comp plan + elections
- Core functions: services, land use, budgets, city manager selection
- Public accountability emphasis

## Takeaways
Budget retreats surface the tension between continuity (two-year cycles) and adaptation (new city manager, comp plan, elections). Mayor's note that "the public really deserves to know what our thinking is" at budget retreats acknowledges these closed-door sessions shape resource allocation more than public hearings on final budgets—transparency requires explaining strategic trade-offs, not just presenting polished numbers.

Diana Langley's first budget retreat coinciding with comprehensive plan work creates natural alignment opportunity: long-term planning should inform near-term spending, not the reverse. The "busy year" acknowledgment suggests council awareness of competing demands but also potential for integrated thinking across traditionally siloed processes.
