# Louisville City Council Special Meeting - July 30, 2024

**Meeting Date:** Tuesday, July 30, 2024, 5:00 PM  
**Meeting Type:** City Council Special Meeting

## Summary
Council held a special meeting focused on urban renewal and economic development initiatives. The primary agenda item was a presentation from the Local Revitalization Commission (LRC) detailing their 12-month progress on downtown vision planning and introducing several new community programs funded through tax increment financing.

## Key Points & Decisions

### Downtown Vision Plan
- **Status**: 12-month development process completed; plan finalizing with consultant team
- **Focus Areas**: Downtown core, Main Street, and French Street
- **Timeline**: Preferred plan adoption targeted before end of 2024; design work expected in 2025; construction beginning 2025
- **Implementation**: Council will review and approve the plan before implementation
- **Community Engagement**: Ongoing business community outreach and assistance during construction phases

### Facade Improvement Program Results
- **Total Awarded**: $504,934.98 in reimbursements
- **Applications**: 21 total applications since 2022 launch
- **Funding Utilization**: 72% of available funding awarded
- **2024 Awards**: $34,000+ in new awards
- **Program Structure**: Tiered matching system (first $15,000 fully funded, then tiered cost-sharing up to 50/50)
- **Economic Impact**: Leveraged approximately $1 million in private business investment into downtown facade improvements
- **Success**: Reduced barrier to entry with initial $15,000 coverage; strong private sector participation

### New Property Improvement Program
- **Purpose**: Provide incentives for interior building rehabilitation within urban renewal area
- **Complement**: Works alongside facade improvement program
- **Goal**: Reduce vacancy and increase vibrancy in downtown district
- **Focus**: Addresses high renovation costs for older and historically designated buildings

### Chase Bank Building (833 Maine) Project
- **Consultant**: Trestle Strategy Group engaged to conduct real estate analysis
- **Deliverable**: Redevelopment and development opportunity analysis for property owner and LRC
- **Timeline**: Contract approval expected within next year
- **Larger Strategy**: Serve as catalyst for broader LRC engagement with property owners interested in URA development
- **Scope**: Address vacant properties and development opportunities

### Oldtown Skate Program
- **LRC Support**: Continued funding for operations and electrical costs for 2024-25 season
- **Prior Investment**: LRC contributed significant capital funding for rink equipment, including skates, in previous year
- **Community Benefit**: Year-round winter activity venue for community

### Tax Increment Financing & Bonding
- **TIFF Expiration**: December 31, 2032
- **Bond Proposal**: $10 million bond for future tax increment revenues
- **Funding Goals**: Support large-scale capital projects including downtown vision implementation and Highway 42/South Street underpass
- **Secondary Benefit**: Maintain programming funding for facade and property improvement programs
- **Timeline**: Final bond terms and amounts to be voted on before end of 2024
- **Council Approval**: Not required for bonding decision but required for project approvals
- **Allocation Requirement**: Funds must be allocated to projects by 2032 deadline or returned to property taxing entities

### Program Review & Incentives
- **Programs**: Facade improvement, property improvement, direct financial assistance, public infrastructure assistance
- **Ongoing Process**: Regular review and assessment of applications for program eligibility and alignment with URA objectives
- **Evaluation Framework**: Programs evaluated against investment goals and downtown revitalization priorities

## Council Input & Directions
- Council expressed appreciation for LRC's work and progress
- LRC requested specific council input on development priorities to ensure alignment during forthcoming bonding and project implementation
- Discussion focused on ensuring city council approval timeline aligns with LRC's project planning and funding allocation needs
- Emphasis on timeline clarity given 2032 TIFF expiration deadline

## Organizational Notes
- **LRC Attendees**: Chair Lexi Adler, Vice Chair Jeff Lipton, Members Barbie Glacius, Cliff Herald (absent - COVID), Bob Tufty (online)
- **City Staff**: Interim City Manager Samma Fox, Community Development Director Rob Zucro, Parks/Recreation Director Adam Blackmore, City Attorney Kathleen Kelly, Interim Deputy City Manager Meredith Muth
- **Council Members Present**: Mayor Lei Prom (Dickinson), Council Members Beerman, Fehee, Hamington, Hoffner, Kern

## Takeaways
1. **Urban Renewal Progress**: LRC has successfully executed multiple programs with significant community/private sector engagement
2. **Financial Leverage**: LRC programs are effectively leveraging private investment; facade program attracted ~$1M private investment
3. **Downtown Revitalization**: Clear multi-year timeline for downtown vision plan adoption and implementation
4. **Strategic Bonding**: $10 million bond will enable major capital projects but requires careful planning given 2032 deadline
5. **Council Alignment**: Both LRC and Council seeking to ensure strategic alignment on development priorities before final bonding and project decisions
