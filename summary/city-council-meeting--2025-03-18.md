# Summary

Louisville City Council conducted routine business including first readings on ADU regulations and family definition zoning amendments, approved landmark designation and grants for a 115-year-old coal miner house, and authorized a $123K wildfire communication campaign. The meeting's contentious element was public comment opposing the inline skating rink conversion to pickle ball courts, citing noise lawsuits in neighboring cities and lack of alternative skating facilities. The mayor pulled the parks/recreation/open space long range plan from consent for discussion, revealing concerns about timeline (17 months, ~30 meetings) and open space representation in the planning process.

## Key Points

- **Inline Rink Controversy:** Multiple residents (including children) opposed pickle ball conversion; cited Lone Tree lawsuit, Centennial moratorium, Denver court removals for noise; staff acknowledged alternative skating locations won't work
- **Ordinances (First Reading, April 15 Hearing):** ADU regulations amendment (1892), family definition zoning update (1893)
- **Lumpty House Landmark:** 509 Lefarge Ave approved for designation; 115-year-old pyramidal hipped roof coal miner housing; French immigrant Leonte family history; $55K grants ($40K restoration + $15K addition + $5K bonus)
- **Wildfire Campaign:** Fors Marsh selected (6 applicants, 3 interviews, unanimous staff choice); $123K repurposed mitigation coordinator grant funds; phased approach (materials → outreach → staff handoff); WOOI code education + fire-wise citywide messaging
- **PROS Long Range Plan:** 17-month timeline for comprehensive plan (actually ~1 year plus pulled Cottonwood piece); ~30 stakeholder meetings; staff nervous timeline aggressive given monthly board schedules; "funnel" approach from comp plan to site-specific; mayor concerned about open space information gaps

## Takeaways

The inline rink pickle ball controversy exposed a recurring Louisville pattern: staff-driven facility changes proceeding without robust public engagement until residents mobilize at council meetings. The rec advisory board's failure to secure alternative skating locations before recommending demolition suggests inadequate due diligence—particularly given the facility's multi-generational user base (kids learning bikes, novice skateboarders, inline hockey players). Helmet Leinski's invocation of Lone Tree lawsuits and Centennial moratoriums signals that Louisville isn't learning from regional precedents on pickle ball noise impacts.

The wildfire communication campaign approval ($123K for Fors Marsh) represents pragmatic grant reallocation from an unfilled mitigation coordinator position toward behavior-change marketing. The 595 consultant hours for phase 1 material development ($57K) raised council questions about scope efficiency, though staff's emphasis on multi-divisional collaboration (open space, community development, communications, recovery/resiliency) and regional partner engagement (wildfire partners, fire district) suggests comprehensive coordination behind the scenes. The challenge will be translating technical fire-wise guidance into compelling neighborhood-level action when the WOOI code arrives.
