## Summary
Council initiated 2026 work plan development in special meeting focused on setting policy priorities beyond day-to-day services. Deputy City Manager Fox clarified work plan items don't require one-year completion (citing multi-year comp plan example), while public comment urged explicit DEI and sustainability columns rather than treating them as "lenses."

## Key Points
- **2026 work plan:** First kickoff meeting, priorities to be finalized later
- **Scope:** Council policy priorities only (not day-to-day services/core operations)
- **Timeline flexibility:** Multi-year projects allowed if actively worked on in 2026
- **Alignment:** Must sync with resident survey, comprehensive plan, budget
- **Strategic framework:** Core services, safety, housing with priority action items
- **Don Parker concern:** DEI & sustainability listed as "lenses" but not showing up in draft priorities
- **Proposal:** Add specific columns for DEI, climate action, biodiversity
- **E-bike safety:** Example of overlooked issue needing work plan attention
- **Process:** Iterative - staff will prepare summaries for council followup

## Takeaways
Don Parker's critique exposes a structural problem in Louisville's work plan design: labeling DEI and sustainability as "lenses" through which to view other priorities risks treating them as optional considerations rather than independent goals. If every priority must be evaluated through these lenses, but neither appears as a standalone item, who ensures they're actually applied? Parker's request for dedicated columns represents a shift from aspirational framing to accountable tracking.

The clarification that work plan items needn't complete within one year addresses Katherine Smith's concern about excluding complex initiatives, but creates ambiguity: how does multi-year inclusion differ from perpetual deferral? The comprehensive plan's multi-year presence suggests legitimacy, but without completion benchmarks, "actively working on it" becomes subjective.

The meeting's iterative approach (initial discussion → staff summaries → council followup) mirrors best practices but frontloads strategic decisions into a process where public engagement is minimal compared to later budget hearings. The 2026 work plan will shape budget priorities, yet this kickoff meeting's sparse public comment suggests limited awareness of its importance.
