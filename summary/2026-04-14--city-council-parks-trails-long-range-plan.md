## Summary
Council used this special meeting as a policy work session on Louisville's next 10-plus-year Parks, Recreation, Open Space, and Trails planning framework. Staff and consultants recapped the second phase of engagement, walked council through draft guiding principles, and presented draft goals for parks, recreation and the senior center, Coal Creek Golf Course, open space, and the city's inaugural trails system plan. The discussion was not a line-edit review; it was a directional checkpoint before staff returns with draft plans in June and a possible final adoption window in late July.

A repeated theme was that the plans need to balance preservation and access without pretending those are simple tradeoffs. Staff described strong overlap across advisory boards and public engagement around deferred maintenance, trail connectivity, safety, sustainability, funding partnerships, and the need for better science-based land-management tools. Council generally supported the structure of the draft goals, but pushed staff to be clearer about what is actually being prioritised, to strengthen references to wildfire mitigation and accessibility, and to avoid muddy phrasing around equity, trail typologies, and open-space protection.

The longest and most detailed conversation centered on trails and open space. Council and public commenters pressed for clearer distinctions between commuter/shared-use paths and softer recreational or open-space trails, stronger treatment of social trails and habitat protection, and more explicit accommodation for residents with different physical abilities. Staff said the draft trails plan would include trail typologies, user expectations, maintenance roles, and standards, while open-space policy would continue to rely on updated data, land classifications, and a future policy for undesignated trails.

## Key Points & Direction

### Project status and timeline
- Staff said the project is in the draft-plan stage after a second round of advisory-board meetings and a second public open house.
- The intent is to bring draft plans back in **June 2026** and target possible final adoption in **late July 2026**.
- This meeting was framed as **discussion and direction**, not detailed wordsmithing.

### Parks and recreation priorities
- For parks, staff highlighted deferred maintenance, missing outdoor courts and multipurpose-field capacity, safer crossings near parks, and stronger neighborhood connections.
- Draft park goals emphasized caring for existing assets first, targeting new amenities based on community need, improving sustainability and resilience, strengthening the urban forest, and improving park connectivity.
- For recreation and the senior center, the major challenges were staffing shortages, demand for youth/senior programming, aquatic-capacity pressure, field conditions, and insufficient sports courts/fields.
- Council also heard that recreation planning needs to serve today's older-skewing population without assuming that demographic mix is permanent.

### Golf, open space, and trails
- Coal Creek Golf Course discussion focused on the undersized clubhouse, maintenance and drainage constraints, restaurant/event potential, and the need to improve long-term financial flexibility.
- Open-space challenges were framed around the tension between conservation and recreational use, the need for better inventories and mapping, the absence of a formal social-trails policy, more education and outreach, and more durable funding.
- Trail connectivity and safety clearly rose to the top in engagement. Staff said the trail plan would cover both soft-surface recreational trails and hard-surface shared-use paths, including clearer standards, maintenance responsibilities, and policy choices around social trails.

### Council feedback
- Several council members liked the draft guiding-principles framework but worried the acronym/ordering could imply priorities that staff did not intend.
- Council pressed for stronger treatment of **wildfire mitigation**, especially in open space and park planning, rather than leaving it as a minor sub-point.
- Accessibility came up repeatedly, especially how to distinguish simple "equal access" from truly equitable access for people with different physical, sensory, or neurodivergent needs.
- Council also asked for more precision around trail categories, where bikes or e-bikes belong, and how different trail types should balance mobility, recreation, habitat, and user safety.

### Public comment themes
- Public commenters supported stronger volunteer opportunities, youth-serving recreation access, and clearer separation between commuter routes and sensitive open-space trails.
- Multiple speakers asked the city to elevate open-space acquisition, habitat connectivity, riparian protection, passive recreation, and the city charter's conservation language more explicitly in the plan.
- Commenters also urged staff to close or better manage undesignated trails in sensitive areas and to avoid overbuilding trail loops around ponds and creeks.

## Takeaways
- This was a substantive planning checkpoint, not a ceremonial update: council gave real direction on accessibility, wildfire, trail classifications, and the preservation-versus-access balance.
- The strongest through-lines were **take care of existing assets**, **improve connectivity and safety**, **use better data**, and **be honest about tradeoffs in open space**.
- The eventual quality of the final plans will depend heavily on whether the June draft sharpens the vague areas council flagged here, especially equity/access language, social-trails policy, and the treatment of habitat protection versus recreation.
