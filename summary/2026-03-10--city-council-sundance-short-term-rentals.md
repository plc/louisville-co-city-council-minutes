# 03/10/26 City Council Special Meeting

## Summary

Louisville City Council recognized winners of the 18th annual Life in Louisville Photography Contest, then gave staff direction for a temporary short-term-rental licensing program tied to the Sundance Film Festival’s move to Boulder. Staff said 74 residents submitted 225 photographs, with 230 votes cast for the People’s Choice award. The winning images will enter the city’s photo repository and may appear in city publications.

The proposed rental framework is intended to help meet regional lodging demand when Sundance begins its Boulder run in January 2027. Council supported allowing apartments with owner permission, applying no new parking or occupancy limits beyond existing rules, coordinating regionally, requiring annual proof of insurance, and renewing licenses annually so taxes and insurance can be checked. Staff will return with a draft ordinance; first reading was tentatively scheduled for April 7 and second reading for April 21.

Council also reviewed a crowded advance agenda. Upcoming work included WUI code, comprehensive-plan adoption, state-required ADU revisions, Marshall Fire rebuild rebates and defensible-space policy, a joint meeting with Superior about a five-year Marshall Fire memorial, and potential Certificates of Participation for the proposed 101 South Taylor purchase.

## Key Points

- The photography contest received 225 entries from 74 residents—75 more photographs than the prior year—and 230 People’s Choice votes.
- Sundance is expected to run in Boulder from 2027 through 2037; staff cited an estimated regional need for 30,000 rooms.
- Staff proposed a $200 application fee, collection of sales and lodging taxes, a life-safety affidavit, complaint-triggered inspections, owner and local contact information, proof of insurance, and visible licensing information in listings.
- Council’s direction was to allow apartments with property-owner approval and impose no additional parking or occupancy limits.
- Council favored annual license renewal and annual proof of insurance, with regional alignment where practical.
- The precise festival window and program name remained to be refined in the draft ordinance.
- There was no public comment on the short-term-rental proposal.
- The Marshall Fire five-year commemoration survey will wait until Louisville and Superior decide whether a joint memorial option can be offered.
- Council anticipated a March 24 reimbursement resolution and May 5/May 19 readings related to financing for 101 South Taylor, with a later go/no-go purchase decision informed by community engagement.

## Decisions and Direction

Council adopted staff’s recap of the short-term-rental direction by consensus: no additional occupancy limits, apartments allowed with owner approval, no additional parking requirements, regional coordination, annual insurance proof, and annual license renewal. Staff was directed to prepare a draft ordinance for later public readings.

## Takeaways

Louisville moved toward a deliberately narrow, festival-focused rental program rather than a general year-round short-term-rental regime. Council emphasized learning from the first Sundance cycle and adjusting later, while avoiding hard-to-enforce parking and occupancy provisions.
