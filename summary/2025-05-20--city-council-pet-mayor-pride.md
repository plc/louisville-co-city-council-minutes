## Summary
Council celebrated the Pet Mayor election winner (Hazel), adopted the Pride Month proclamation with Mayor Pro Tem Dickinson's personal reflection on his three queer children, and approved Memorial Day observance and budget amendment resolutions. The meeting blended community-building traditions with affirming LGBTQ+ inclusion amid national discrimination concerns.

## Key Points
- **Pet Mayor winner:** Hazel (great campaigner, strong on pet policy)
- **Pride Month proclamation:** Progress Pride flag to fly throughout June 2025
- **Dickinson family:** All three children queer (bisexual, lesbian, non-binary)
- **Proclamation emphasis:** Visibility, advocacy, education vital for safe discrimination-free community
- **Context:** "efforts to undermine the rights and liberties of LGBTQ plus people nationwide have continued"
- **Memorial Day observance:** Commemorating 1868 origins as Decoration Day
- **Budget resolutions:** 30 & 31 (2025 budget amendments) approved after public hearing

## Takeaways
Mayor Pro Tem Dickinson's proclamation reading personalized Louisville's Pride commitment beyond symbolic gesture—his reflection on parenting three queer children anchored abstract inclusion language in lived experience. The proclamation's acknowledgment that "efforts to undermine the rights and liberties of LGBTQ plus people nationwide have continued" signals Louisville's awareness of 2025 political climate while affirming local protections.

The Pet Mayor tradition (started post-Marshall Fire) continues maturing as community-building ritual. Hazel's campaign prowess ("way with people," "understands policies") playfully mirrors real electoral qualities while creating low-stakes civic engagement for families. The juxtaposition Mayor Leiker noted—"from very clear hate speech to wonderful pet mayor moment to pride proclamation"—suggests the meeting navigated challenging public comment before these celebratory items.
