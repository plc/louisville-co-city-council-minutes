## Summary
Council met with the 16-member Youth Advisory Board to review their work on the Pet Mayor community-building project (voting underway) and All Careers environmental sustainability program. Council encouraged youth to engage more directly in policy decisions affecting them, particularly comprehensive planning, downtown vision, and e-bike/e-scooter safety regulations.

## Key Points
- **Youth Advisory Board:** 16 members (middle school to high school), some serving up to 6 years
- **Pet Mayor election:** Top 10 finalists, voting in progress, meet-and-greet scheduled, announcement May 2
- **All Careers program:** Sustainability across all professions (not just environmental careers), led by educator Martin Ogle
- **Field trips:** Jack's (Lafayette), Alpin Windows (Louisville-exclusive window manufacturing)
- **Sunday event:** Student speakers + sustainable business owners presenting All Careers model (public invited)
- **Sweetheart Dance:** Board volunteered (February), ran greeting/food/games/crafts
- **Youth priorities:** Green roofs, parks/open space maintenance, sustainable development (solar), dark skies, walkability
- **Council encouragement:** Youth voices needed on comp plan, downtown vision, e-bike/e-scooter policy
- **Work plan engagement:** Youth invited to weigh in on summer budget discussions
- **Board autonomy:** Freedom to choose projects (last year: Eco Seekers; this year: Pet Mayor)

## Takeaways
The Youth Advisory Board stands out as Louisville's longest-tenured board members—some students have served six years, outlasting most elected officials. Their Pet Mayor project exemplifies Louisville's community-building ethos, with Council Member Hamlington noting the posters at the rec center make her smile daily and embody "why Lewisville is so awesome."

The All Careers program represents sophisticated environmental thinking beyond traditional green jobs, emphasizing sustainability across all professions. A sixth-grader's perspective shift after attending last year's event demonstrates the program's impact across age groups. Alpin Windows' Louisville-exclusive manufacturing provided a tangible local example of innovation and sustainability.

Council's invitation for youth engagement on policy issues like comprehensive planning and e-bike regulations acknowledges a gap—these decisions will shape Louisville over the next decade, yet the people most affected (current youth) rarely have formal input mechanisms. The board's interest in green roofs, dark skies, and walkability suggests youth priorities align with sustainability lenses in the council work plan but with more specific, tangible ideas.

The meeting's casual format (council sitting "on the floor at eye level") and Council Member Kiernan's illness-induced silence created unusual intimacy and youth-led discussion dynamics.
