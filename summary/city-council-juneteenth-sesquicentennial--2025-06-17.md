## Summary
Council observed Juneteenth with proclamation read by Mayor Pro Tem Dickinson and accepted Louisville's sesquicentennial (150th anniversary) proclamation. Both ceremonial items approved in regular meeting.

## Key Points
- **Juneteenth proclamation:** Commemorating Emancipation Proclamation (Jan 1, 1863) and June 19 observance
- **Read by:** Mayor Pro Tem Dickinson
- **Sesquicentennial:** Louisville's 150th anniversary recognition
- Both proclamations approved

## Takeaways
The pairing of Juneteenth and sesquicentennial proclamations invites reflection on overlapping timelines—Louisville's 150-year history (founded 1875) began just 12 years after the Emancipation Proclamation and 10 years after Juneteenth (1865). The city's founding occurred during Reconstruction, a context rarely foregrounded in sesquicentennial celebrations but relevant to understanding Louisville's early development amid profound national transformation on race and citizenship.
