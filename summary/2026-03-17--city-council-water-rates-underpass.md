# 03/17/26 City Council Special Meeting

## Summary

Louisville City Council opened with an executive session on a potential disposition of city-owned real property. After returning to open session, council voted to accept the reviewed offer, subject to adoption and final effectiveness of an ordinance authorizing the conveyance. The city attorney was directed to draft that ordinance for the March 24 meeting.

The main workshop covered water and sewer rates and tap fees. Staff described Louisville's diversified water portfolio, the completed Chimney Hollow Reservoir project, conservation programs, the cost of maintaining treatment and distribution systems, and the tension between stable revenue and conservation incentives. Council asked the Finance Committee and staff to examine a higher base charge paired with usage-based billing, steeper inclining blocks above ordinary indoor and responsible irrigation use, possible seasonal rates, drought surcharges, and payment plans or surcharges that could finance water tap fees for qualifying development projects. Staff emphasized that tap fees support growth-related capacity and that wastewater expansion for Redtail remains directly funded under existing agreements.

Council then approved direction for the South Street pedestrian/bicycle underpass at Highway 42. Staff's revised “A1” concept preserves Tebo Properties' developable pad and relocates a right-in/right-out driveway to align with existing parking circulation. Council directed staff to pursue A1 only if the property owner pays for the driveway and withdraws opposition or otherwise supports the project. If an agreement cannot be reached promptly, staff may pivot without returning to council to Option B, which stays outside the private property, so the city does not jeopardize grant funding or lose more time to construction-cost escalation.

## Key Points

- Council accepted an offer for a potential city-property disposition, contingent on a later ordinance authorizing the conveyance.
- Louisville uses roughly one billion gallons of water annually; staff said conservation remains cheaper than acquiring and building equivalent new supply.
- Staff identified revenue stability as a priority because current base charges do not fully cover fixed utility costs and summer use supplies a disproportionate share of revenue.
- Council supported studying a revised base fee, billing from the first units of consumption, steeper high-use tiers, seasonal pricing, and drought surcharges while keeping essential indoor water affordable.
- Council asked staff to continue exploring tap-fee financing tools, particularly where water-fund financing could help otherwise viable development without shifting growth costs to existing customers.
- Public commenter Betty Solik raised water waste from an irrigated Centennial Parkway median; staff said drought response would combine outreach to large users, escalating rates, and code enforcement when necessary.
- The revised South Street underpass concept is estimated to add roughly $800,000 to $1 million in design complexity, plus hundreds of thousands from delay and escalation.
- Council's fallback authority lets staff move to Option B if Tebo Properties will not cost-share and drop its opposition, protecting the project's grant and schedule.

## Decisions and Direction

1. Accept the reviewed real-property offer, conditioned on a future ordinance authorizing conveyance; direct the city attorney to prepare the ordinance.
2. Send utility-rate stabilization, conservation pricing, seasonal/drought tools, and tap-fee financing options to further staff and Finance Committee analysis.
3. Advance South Street underpass Option A1 only with property-owner cost sharing and support/non-opposition; otherwise move directly to Option B without another council decision.

## Public Comment

One resident described frequent runoff from an irrigated grass median on Centennial Parkway. Staff explained that landscape-only taps are billed under the existing system and said the city would use higher-use pricing, drought outreach, and enforcement to address persistent waste.

## Takeaways

The meeting paired long-term utility resilience with a practical attempt to keep rates predictable and conservation-oriented. On the underpass, council chose a firm two-track strategy: seek the more integrated A1 design, but do not sacrifice grant funding or schedule if the private-property agreement cannot be secured.
