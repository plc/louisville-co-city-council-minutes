# Summary

Louisville City Council received comprehensive presentations on police department operations and municipal cybersecurity posture. The police department highlighted achieving full staffing for the first time in years (12 new hires in 2024), implementing new management systems (Frontline), and earning CACP reaccreditation. However, Chief Rafer candidly acknowledged Louisville ranks "last in our region" among ~40 agencies for officer compensation, losing experienced officers at step 3-4 of the six-step pay scale. The cybersecurity presentation revealed ongoing vulnerability management challenges—4,310 vulnerabilities with 2,200 remaining, rising faster than mitigation efforts—though recent improvements include SCADA system isolation (separating water/wastewater controls from main network) and Arctic Wolf 24/7 monitoring partnership.

## Key Points

- **Staffing Milestone:** Full staffing achieved December 2024 after hiring 12 employees; currently down one officer (left for another state)
- **Compensation Crisis:** Louisville ranks "bottom eight" in regional compensation survey of 40 agencies; competitive at entry level but significantly behind at steps 3-4 through top end of six-step scale
- **2024 Accomplishments:** CACP reaccreditation, Frontline web-based management system, e-bike patrol (3 bikes, 3 officers + 2 rangers certified), $320K in federal/state grants, platoon scheduling with Wednesday training days
- **2025 Goals:** 5% increase in enforcement contacts, 5% increase in community events (from 62 in 2024), "Shield Initiative" community policing model, auto theft focus, 90% satisfaction rating target (up from 87%)
- **Department Structure:** 35 sworn officers (28 patrol, 4 detectives), 5.5 support staff, ~45 total employees; detective bureau handles ~15 new cases/month
- **Property & Evidence:** Clean annual audit of 1,566 items (1,177 intake, 137 disposed, 252 returned); records unit processed 573 public records requests including growing body cam redaction workload
- **Co-Responder Program:** Mental health calls consistently increased month-over-month since April 2024 launch
- **Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities:** 4,310 identified, 1,875 mitigated, 2,200 remaining; vulnerabilities accumulating faster than remediation capacity
- **Security Improvements:** SCADA isolation (water/wastewater systems no longer connected to main domain), multi-factor authentication reimplementation, .us.gov email transition
- **Security Partners:** Arctic Wolf (24/7 managed SOC), Rule 4 (local Boulder County incident response), CIPA (Colorado grant funding, provided CrowdStrike), MS-ISAC (zero-day alerts), Seagate (state IT collaboration)

## Takeaways

The police department's operational success story—full staffing, reaccreditation, innovative programs—is undermined by a compensation structure that virtually guarantees losing trained officers to higher-paying neighbors. Chief Rafer's transparent admission that Louisville is "last in our region" and can only compete on "quality of life" rather than salary suggests a looming retention crisis once the 2024 hiring cohort reaches year three or four. The decision not to join the county drug task force due to staffing constraints illustrates how baseline capacity gaps foreclose specialized opportunities, creating a vicious cycle where Louisville trains officers for wealthier departments.

The cybersecurity presentation revealed a more nuanced risk picture than typical government assurances. The IT director's candor—vulnerabilities accumulating faster than remediation, 2,200 remaining items requiring individual machine-level review, Arctic Wolf sensors detecting threats 24/7 because "actors do not sleep"—paints a picture of constant triage rather than comprehensive defense. The SCADA isolation achievement stands out as critical: separating water and wastewater plant controls from the main network eliminates a catastrophic attack vector that existed as recently as two years ago. However, the broader vulnerability backlog suggests Louisville, like most municipalities, is perpetually behind the threat curve, relying on partnership networks (Rule 4, CIPA, MS-ISAC) to stay minimally resilient rather than achieving true security maturity.
