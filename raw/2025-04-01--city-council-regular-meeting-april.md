# Louisville City Council Regular Meeting
**Date:** April 1, 2025

## Key Actions

### New City Manager Welcome
>> The meeting opened with a welcome to new city manager Diane Langley. Mayor Leiker stated: "we've just finished with a Welcome to our new city manager Diane Langley."

>> City Manager Langley responded: "first of all I want to say thank you to council um everybody has been so welcoming and I'm really looking forward to being City Manager for Louisville."

### Public Comment - Climate Change & Block Party Requirements

>> Don Parker (resident of 11 years) raised climate concerns: "I'm really concerned about climate change and loss of biodiversity so I would ask that you do whatever you can to uh to address those issues and uh particularly may make Lewisville a model for other communities and cities in that regard."

>> Parker proposed: "one of my pet things would be to close the golf course and we wild it so you can keep that in mind not may not be popular."

>> Parker also questioned block party permit requirements: "the city website it says you have to submit an application 21 days in advance which I I don't know what why it has to be so far in advance I'd like to look 10 days in advance look at the weather so I wonder if we could look at that requirement."

>> Parker noted permit website gap: "the website says that event sponsors who hold an event without a permit will not be eligible for a permit for 12 months but it doesn't doesn't say when a permit is required for a block party."

## Utility Rates Ordinance & Resolution

### Ordinance 1894 - Water to Wastewater Loan
>> Title: "an ordinance authorizing a loan from the Water Utility Fund to the Wastewater Utility Fund for a three-year term and an initial interest rate of 4.8%."

The ordinance passed on first reading to set public hearing for April 15, 2025. Roll call vote: all yes.

### Resolution 23 - Utility Rate Adjustments

>> Public Works Director Kirk Copher presented: "tonight we'll be talking about your utility rates uh being proposed for 2025... this is a process that we start every year um around June July and we work through several meetings with the finance committee and the finance department."

>> 2024 water fund performance: "we had a really dry Year and that results in more revenue for the utility on the water side so the water fund was about 10% over targets by $726,000."

>> Context on revenue variability: "previous years we had very wet weather so we're very behind on revenues so seasonally we can be up and down in our overall swing in Colorado as climate impacts seems to be around 30 to 40% so our extremes are getting more so."
