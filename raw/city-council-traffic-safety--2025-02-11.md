### Vision Zero & Safe System Approach

>> Jeff Nettleton, Civil Engineer 3: "We're committed to eliminating serious injuries, accidents, fatalities, and over the next several slides, we'll kind of see what that vision might look like. So, zero is our goal and a safe system is there."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "What FHWA is now moving forward with. It's a safe system approach. It's about being proactive. The aim is to eliminate traffic fatalities, serious injuries. Objectives from this are safer people, safer roads, safer speeds, safer vehicles, and post-crash care."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Some of the guiding principles being death and serious injuries are unacceptable. That's the primary goal. It recognizes that humans make mistakes, they're vulnerable, that the responsibility is shared among all users. Safety is proactive. We try to be proactive. We try to get some engagement in the community of what is happening and try to get ahead of it. And then redundancy. Redundancy is crucial."

### Terminology

>> Jeff Nettleton: "When we discuss the 85th percentile, that's the speed at which or below 85% of drivers are traveling. It's a metric that we tend to use for gauging. It's an industry standard throughout and it's a gauge for vehicular behavior and observation of traffic patterns."

*Council member clarified: "Is that the middle or is 15% above? So 85th and below will be what we measure. 15% going to be excess."*

### Colorado Traffic Laws

>> Jeff Nettleton: "In summary, the first one, the expectation is that drivers stop to wait to crosswalks when there's a pedestrian present showing intent. And then that second bullet there is that pedestrians and bicycles shall be prepared to stop at all crossings."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Drivers stop and wait at crosswalks. Very important. Colorado law recognizes that crosswalks exist at intersections and they don't need to be marked. If you're in an intersection, you're making your way across the street that is a crosswalk."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Per CDOT, every pedestrian crossing a roadway at any point other than within a marked crosswalk or with an unmarked intersection shall yield the right of way to all vehicles upon entering a road. An example of that would be in downtown where we see a lot of people coming out of a business getting parked on the other side of the car or on the other side of Main Street maybe and they don't want to go to the crosswalk."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "One thing to note just on the hawks and the RFBs, we do not recommend that they get mixed on the same corridor."

## Focus Areas & Evaluation Criteria

### Three E's Framework

>> Jeff Nettleton: "We call it three E. It's like a three-legged stool and that's the education and enforcement and engineering."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Education we all go get our driver's license test. We all need to know how to operate a vehicle. We all have to make sure we're making good decisions behind the wheels following the traffic laws. We have new independent users every year coming up and growing up through our school systems and we take for granted sometimes that they just were born knowing how to operate in the street. They don't. I've got I've got a 12-year-old, two eight-year-olds that live this world and they still don't understand. Their brains aren't fully developed. We have to work with that."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Enforcement, people make decisions. Humans make decisions. We've all probably made a choice at some point to drive over the speed limit. I have lived that. I don't love it. It happens. Sometimes, not even on purpose. Sometimes you're just driving along, lost in a train of thought, listening to whatever it is. You look up and you're comfortably driving and it's 30 and you're going 40. So you just try to make sure that the enforcement is there to make sure the community respects the rules that exist."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Then of course the engineering we implement infrastructure. That's what our group is here to do. We take those complaints. We see if there's some additional pieces to the first two E's that we can better implement."

### Evaluation Factors

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Volume, how much traffic does that particular road see? Posted speed limit versus the 85th percentile. So, again, if we're posted 30, you're seeing the 85th percentile, majority of those cars driving that 38, we've got probably some speeding issues out there. So, then we look at enforcement. They look at additional improvements from engineering perspective."

>> Jeff Nettleton: "We look at schools, regional trails, vulnerable users, other factors. ADT average daily trips. And then another competing interest, police and fire delay response times. What do those look like? If we make some improvements, do we create problems there?"

>> Jeff Nettleton: "Accident history patterns. What are the causes of accidents? Context of the road uses residential, commercial, industrial. And then again, we have residential on street parking. It's very important to our residents out there. And then of course, the last piece that we like to consider is community. How much what's the comfort level to feel within that little micro area?"
