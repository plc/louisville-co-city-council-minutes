# Louisville City Council Joint Cultural Advisory Board Meeting
**Date:** May 13, 2025

## Public Comment - Leah Franks (Friends of Louisville Arts, Sciences & Humanities)

>> Background: "I served on the cultural council for five years. Um and I am an alum of last year's cult uh Colorado business committee for the arts cohort and I am currently the chair of a nonprofit in Lewisville called Friends of Lewisville Arts, Sciences, and Humanities. We go by Flash."

>> Flash mission: "our vision is to strategically unify local organizations around our shared goals of um, cultural and art advocacy."

>> Arts importance: "it is now more important than ever to prioritize support for the arts in Lewisville. Art unifies communities, drives tourism, economic vitality, creates jobs, leads to civic engagement, and helps understand other cultures."

>> Budget concerns: "with the recent cuts to the cultural services budget and cancellation of millions of dollars of federal arts and humanities grants, uh, I wanted to encourage the council to utilize the folks on the cultural advisory board as efficiently as possible."

>> Colorado context: "Colorado is the number one uh state in participation in the arts but one of the worst at uh city or government funding for the arts... cultural programming funds come from SCFD and with an uncertain economic future that's concerning."

>> Performing arts space needs: "Lewisville lacks any accessible or inclusive performing arts spaces. Um I'm enthused to hear about potential upgrades to Steinbot Pavilion... Flash has already started the process of meeting with city staff, um, other community organizations like Center Stage, other m municipalities like Northline Arts, and the folks behind Create Boulder to start a conversation about maybe building a new inclusive performing arts space in Lewisville."

## Cultural Advisory Board Role & History

>> CAB Chair J.R. Kettlesson explained transition: "last year we became an advisory board. Before that, you know, I don't know what our I don't know what our description was, but we were not in an advisory role. We were actually in kind of like a voting role where we had to have a quorum u and we had to vote on anything that was like more than $500."

>> Previous process: "all the entertainment that happened like concerts in the park um you know anything that we did at the center for the arts that was more than $500 for an artist we had to get together we had to have a meeting and we had to um vote on that and have a majority approve that."

>> Impact of change: "Since we've become an advisory board, I just want to point out how much more efficient it is and how much, you know, I, you know, was skeptical at first, but uh now I will tell you that working with Iris and John has been a real pleasure and uh we just get a lot more stuff done now than we did before."

### CAB Advisory Responsibilities

>> Staff presentation: "the cultural advisory board is advisory to the city council. Um it provides an an advisory capacity to the city council on matters related to the arts and culture."

>> Specific duties: "CAB advises the city council and staff in connection with cultural programming, the future development of arts and culture in Lewisville, and matters that support the equal rights and opportunity to participate in culture. Um, advise on the allocation of funds and support of the arts. advise in matters relating to the city's cultural arts master plan, including advising on master plan updates in medium and long-term planning for arts and events and plan and execute an annual outdoor concert series."

## 2024 Highlights

### Public Art Projects
>> Three installations: "we advised on development and installation of three public art projects. They included the mural that's out front um on the the pollinator mural, the um the Marshall fire pet memorial that happened um over in the arboritum and then the uh community resilience sculpture that got installed right out in front of city hall."

>> Delayed project: "we had um funding and we were going to fund a mural at Steinball, but there were some issues, you know, structural issues at Steinball. So, we had to we had we weren't able to move forward with that. We'd love to be able to move forward with some of that funding this year uh for um public art installations."

### Events Programming

>> Overall achievement: "we advised, strategized and volunteered actively on um arts and entertainment's most dynamic and complete year of events... based on coming out of this this board."

>> Event totals: "last year there are 27 total events. 23 were one-off events. Those one-off events are things like the um uh block party that we have, uh the movie on Main Street that we have, the concerts in the park, and all of the music that we do at the Center for the Arts."

>> Attendance: "we had over 20,000 people you know attended those events."

>> Diversity noted: "we had a lot of diversity in our in our uh presentations that we had in our events."

### Summer Concerts in the Park
>> 2024 performance: "we are responsible for um finding finding the talent and booking the talent and the sound for the summer concerts in the park. Um we do that. Last year we did five of those summer concerts."

>> 2025 budget constraints: "This year price is going up. We're only going to be able to do four. We'd love to be able to do more."

>> Attendance estimate: "They were all well attended. It was probably we think it was between 750 and 1,200 people were at each one of those events."

### Art Grants Program
>> Award structure: "We do an art grant program where we have five art grants that are worth $2,000 a piece and uh they have to be done in Lewisville."

>> Application volume: "people will apply for those and then as a board we will go through those... We usually get between 15 and 20 uh applications for that."

>> Process improvement: "we also developed a new scoring matrix for that to make it easier for the board members and um you know to so they could understand you know uh truly if you're looking at uh EDI as a criterion then what does that truly mean to be a 1 2 3 4 or five and we've got the matrix uh built out for that."

>> EDI examples: "Bingo's Birthday, which was a play geared towards uh children... transformative stories uh was another one. That was last year was another one that we did last year. Uh transformative stories was stories about uh trans individuals, you know, the trans community."

### New Cultural Traditions

>> Block party evolution: "one of the things we started last year is we started establishing some new cultural traditions in Lewisville. An example of that is the block party that started right after the Marshall fire and um it was done, the first block party was done over at the fire station across from the rec center. And then two years ago, uh that came to uh the downtown fire station, fire station number one."

>> Partnership change: "Um this year we're going to do that again. And so this will be our third, this will be our fourth year, actually our fourth year this year. And this year um the city we're doing that on our on our own um to to bring that uh that that event together."

>> 2024 enhancements: "Last year it was stepped up uh to a higher level. We had we actually had tables people could sit at. Um we had food trucks. We had more food trucks there. We had entertainment for children. it truly became like a community event."

>> Upcoming: "that's coming up you know in the in the near future. It's at the June 8th."

>> Movie on Main: "the other thing we did uh another new tradition that we started last year was the movie on Maine... moving on Maine, I think also is something that will, um, lend itself to help with like economic vitality."

## Notes
This special meeting focused on the Cultural Advisory Board's 2024 achievements and transition to advisory (vs. voting) role. The meeting highlighted efficiency gains from the structural change and record-breaking event programming (27 events, 20,000+ attendees). Public comment emphasized need for inclusive performing arts space and concerns about federal arts funding cuts.
