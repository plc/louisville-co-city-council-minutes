# Louisville City Council Special Meeting: 2025 Work Plan
January 14, 2025

## Public Comment

### Wildfire Mitigation & Emergency Preparedness

>> Ann Brennan, 888 Larksburg Court: "I sent an email to the council over the weekend about fire mitigation, wildfire protection, and that I didn't see that in the plan. I know the city and the county have made a lot of progress. We've got the CWP came out this fall, but of course that's at a county level that's not specific to Lewisville. There's some great detail about open space, but otherwise there a lot of placeholders it seems like for city planning. We have been told over the last year that the city would follow up with a plan after this came out. So that seems like that needs to be a priority and something the council really needs to engage on and direct city staff to make a priority this year for the whole city."

>> Ann Brennan: "We have this risk assessment that was done in 2023 but this does not address the whole city. This addresses by design city-owned land and city infrastructure. There's to my knowledge there's no plan for the whole city including the residences and businesses that make up the city and this was a real disappointment to the people who lost their homes is that the planning that happened didn't include those of us the kinds of losses that we sustained after the Marshall fire."

>> Ann Brennan: "The third area that I think we need to focus is emergency preparedness. I don't see that represented anywhere on the website. There have been a lot of discussion online since the LA fires about whether Lewisville has evacuation planning and what does that look like and I don't see that anywhere on the website."

>> Larry Donner, 1020 Willow Place: "I've read the city work plans to be discussed tonight and I applaud the continuing emphasis on public safety and core services. However, I would like to warn against complacency. It's not enough to have a written study on wildfire risk. Response plans need to be written and practiced in the field. Basic utilities such as our water system need to be maintained and tested on a regular basis. We cannot assume studies alone will avoid future disasters."

>> Larry Donner: "We need to have and practice disaster responses for any largescale event that may strike the city. Secondly, we need to turn potential partnerships into real functional partnerships. It isn't enough to have a potential partner regarding the office of disaster management. We need to be an active partner participating in activations and planning for events countywide."

>> Larry Donner: "The ongoing disaster in Los Angeles unfortunately serves to underscore my concerns. LA had plans in place, but they lost focus, and now they're paying dearly for their complacency. We can and must avoid a similar loss of focus here in Lewisville."

### Affordable Housing Partnerships

There are a number of nonprofit housing providers working in the affordable housing space locally, regionally, statewide, even nationally that could really come to become very important partners in any of the Lewisville affordable housing efforts."

>> Michael Reese: "Wherever we can put more color on just above the potential partners section, any time we can put more color and some actual tasks that will be expedited in the review process so that the development community can sort of garner what expedited review really means as they're scheduling out their particular projects."

### Work Plan Lenses Concern

>> Parker, 378 GR Court: "I was a little disappointed with the work plan. It seems like the lenses are actually like lip service because we don't see sustainability or EDI anywhere in the plan. So there's a lens but doesn't seem like so also seem like there isn't much detail enough detail in there specifically for example the economic vitality it seemed like there should be something there about marketing getting the word out to other communities to come visit Lewisville."

## Mayor's Opening Remarks

>> Mayor: "The work plan identifies the projects and policies that require city council input or meeting time. It does not include the day-to-day work of staff. The priorities in the draft were agreed on last year and the goal tonight is for council review and give feedback on the draft."

>> Mayor: "The work plan reflecting council works only. We've had discussions in the past about well wait a second aren't core services part of the council priorities. Well of course they are. That's why we call them core services. But they happen whether well we intend that they happen regardless of any of the other priorities that we have."

>> Mayor: "Certainly the council work plan is designed should be achievable. I think it's important even before it's achievable to try our very best to be as specific as it needs to be but not more. We need to be clear about what policies are of priority priorities for this year. Those need to be articulated well."

>> Mayor: "Part of the disconnect that we have sometimes had is disconnect between council and its boards and commissions. Sometimes we have disconnect council and staff, but I think we've kind of got staff covered because I think that that's been important part of this. But I think boards and commissions have been confused at times. So, we really need to make sure that we are clear because our priorities are going to be the ones that we will expect to be followed."

>> Mayor: "It is our responsibility. We direct city manager to carry out the policy agenda that we articulate and lest anyone have passed lest anyone think that somehow this is staff running away with policy that is not the intention or the goal."

## Staff Presentation: Strategic Framework

>> Sam Fox, Interim City Manager: "Tonight, we have our whole executive leadership team here. As I think everyone knows, nothing happens in a vacuum. It takes a lot of people to have the city function. And so, this team has been really vital as they were in the budget process to helping flesh out the work plan."

>> Sam Fox: "This is intended to be council's 2025 priority. It is above and beyond day-to-day. It is council's work plan. So we want to make sure that it works for you. It's at that policy level. It should be achievable. It could be adjusted. This work plan is not written in stone. Changes throughout the year. We can adjust that."

>> Sam Fox: "Changes do have impacts. So if we do have injections or new needs that come up, that may mean we have to shift some of our priorities. That's something that we'll endeavor to bring to you as staff with alternatives so that you can make an informed policy decision about priorities that come up."

### Hierarchy of Needs

>> Sam Fox: "I do want to touch base on the hierarchy of needs. I think this council has been really receptive and understanding to this. I think it's really important that our core services, our base operating needs, clean water is foundational to all of these things. There are often urgent and critical needs. There's community demand and there's aspirational."

>> Sam Fox: "Louisville has been really blessed to spend a lot of time in aspirational and that's amazing and something that we need to do if we want to continue being aspirational is make sure that we're taking care of our services."

>> Sam Fox: "I think something else as I reflect on my shorter tenure with the city of Louisville is we've done a lot. We've come through COVID we've come through a fire. We've gotten a little bit good at doing more with less and sometimes that's not an achievable model. We want to be really thoughtful that we're resourcing appropriately and making very intentional service level decisions."

### Timeline & Process

>> Sam Fox: "One of the things that happened two years ago is intentionally moving the start and work planning conversations before the budget. This year with so many things lining up, we made sure to have comprehensive plan conversations. We made sure to have community survey result conversations and use this work plan setting and priorities to inform those budget decisions."

## Four Strategic Priorities

>> Sam Fox: "It's no surprise your four priorities as identified currently are economic vitality, core services, safety, affordable housing with two lenses applied through everything of equity, diversity, inclusion, and environment."

### Guiding Documents

>> Sam Fox: "I do want to highlight our guiding documents. First and foremost, we have our charter as a home municipality. We'll always be looking to that. We have our code. We have our adopted 25 and 26 budget. We have our community survey which we do every four years. We're fortunate to do in 2024 and our comprehensive plan. And when our 2025 comprehensive plan is adopted, we'll move to that."

### Application of Lenses

>> Sam Fox: "These lenses are meant to be applied throughout every framework. So, as we think about our pavement program, are we applying that equitably throughout the community? Are we doing it in an environmentally sustainable way? I think one of the things that we'll try to work through this year is making sure that in our communications to council, we are highlighting how these lenses are applied so you can understand it."

## Priority 1: Economic Vitality

>> Sam Fox: "What we heard from council is that economic vitality, sometimes known as economic prosperity, includes completion of current planning initiatives such as the comprehensive plan, the downtown vision plan, code updates to continue businessfriendly approach, and traditional economic development focuses such as business attraction and retention."

### Key Projects

>> Sam Fox: "Not surprisingly, our first projects are the comprehensive plan and the downtown vision plan, which are identified in the description. We also believe that the economic vitality committee from council will likely have some policy recommendations. So, we've placed that here."

>> Sam Fox: "We've heard from council a desire for some post2025 comprehensive plan initiatives including potentially targeted development code amendments and a development code review."

>> Sam Fox: "This doesn't mean we don't do traditional business attraction, business retention, the work we're doing building relationships, those are going to be continuing throughout as a staff item continue to go."

### Potential Partners

>> Sam Fox: "Some of our partners that we'll likely work with on this that we are working with in a lot of ways are Louisville Chamber, Downtown Business Association, and the Louisville Revitalization."
