# Louisville City Council Regular Meeting - PROS Contract Debate
**Date:** May 6, 2025

## Public Comments - Parks, Recreation & Open Space Contract

### Katherine Smith (Ward 3)
>> Request: "asking you to take the award of the pros contract off of the consent agenda is a hard thing for you to do, but I think it's an important thing for you to do."

>> Allocation concerns: "there's a historical pattern of using the open space as a slush fund to fund the expenses of other divisions. And what we have from the staff in the latest memo in response to your request for clarification of the allocation is an explanation of where the money comes from. But where the money comes from is not a justification for how they're going to how the money is going to be spent. Once it is open spaces money, it should be spent on open space."

>> Foundation problems: "Vigilance can't fix a trails plan that has been formed on an incorrect foundational basis. We need to know where the good habitat is, where the species of special concern are living, where are the wild flowers. And once we know that, we can decide where our trails belong."

>> Contractual deficiencies: "there is no defined to deliverable for open space, which pretty much means that whatever the consultant gives you is what you're going to live with because you haven't spelled out what it is you're expecting. The contract doesn't say what the subcontractor erro will do. So again, whatever they do is going to be okay. The contract doesn't even say that DTJ has to use erro and that is was a precondition of open spaces agreeing to this particular setup."

>> Recommendation: "The best course of action is for you to give the staff guidance have them return at the May 20th meeting and let's get this done. If there's something that you want LOSA to do we are more than happy to do it."

### Tamar Krauss (Lewisville resident)

>> Property acquisition request: "I wanted to ask you if you could do everything in your power to purchase the property at 96 in Dylan. It was on the map of the open space advisory board recommended candidate properties for acquisition and it's bordered on three sides by other open space properties. So that would make it a really really important for habitat habitat connectivity."

>> Contract concerns: "I wanted to reiterate what Katherine just said um and ask you to please take the parks, recreation, and open space department long range plan and trails master plan contract off of your consent agenda."

>> Missing elements: "as you know, our open space management plan was last updated in 2004, and we really are in need of having this done... reminding you to look at the exclusions. It's spec it specifically excludes an open space, wildlife, natural resources, archaeological and habitat inventory and sight specific management and maintenance plans. And it specifically excludes field ecological inventory or open space management plan. Um, and it excludes native prairie restoration plans and procedures."

### Matt Jones (Ward 3, Dutch Creek)

>> Background: "I used to be an environmental planner. Did it for 18 years at City of Boulder Open Space and Mountain Parks."

>> Concerns: "I see are two problems coming at you if you don't have staff go back and make this thing more specific, especially on the environmental side, the ecological side. I think there will be poor decisions that get made on both the the parks, recreation, and open space plan and the trails plan."

>> Long-term impacts: "you know, we're talking about doing an open space plan later. It's the master plan. It's going to be hard to overrule what's in it. And so, you need to get this this right in my opinion."

>> Community conflict warning: "when those decisions get made, I think it will cause conflict in the community. I needless conflict... you're going to spend more time and there'll be more conflict in the community if you don't do the thing right in the first place."

>> Criticism of staff response: "you asked staff to do that. I was disappointed. There were some changes in this in the memo, but I was disappointed in how little it had changed and how little specifics the RRO the environmental consultant had in the contract. You don't know what they're doing. I don't know what they're doing."

>> Request: "Can you please ask staff to redo the memo so that it is explicit and we have a smooth process where we can constructively figure out what we need to do these projects."

## Ordinance 1895 - Contractor Insurance Requirements Amendment (First Reading)

### Process Change
>> City Manager: "past practice has been that there's a first reading in which there's not a presentation by council and then the public hearing is set and then during the second reading is when there's a presentation by uh staff... we're changing the process so that staff does a presentation as part of the first reading to not only introduce the topic to council but then also to the public."

>> Rationale: "That then provides uh the two weeks um in between the first reading and the second reading for staff or the public to ask additional questions, council to get clarification or if something just really strikes council as wrong as part of the first reading, um we can go back to the drawing board without setting the public hearing."

### Rob Zukero, Community Development Director Presentation

>> Background: "the city began, I believe, contractor licensing in 2016. The ordinance that we have now that um dictates how we do contractor licensing was last updated in 2013."

>> Proposed amendments:
1. "allowing that the city verify um for the city to verify that contractors hold general liability insurance. Right now, our ordinance says that when you apply for a contractor license, you have to attest when you sign, but it doesn't say anything beyond that."

2. "update the licensing test requirements to the 2018 code series or better."

3. "our building inspectors or our building official could require that a licensed supervisor... if the city um deems it necessary, we would like to have the ability to require that supervisor on site for inspections."

4. "require that all state licensed electrical and plumbing contractors have their license on site with them. This is actually a state requirement. They're asking us to verify when we're doing inspections."

5. "authorize the building official to issue stop work orders if there is a violation of the licensing requirements."

### Council Discussion

>> Mayor Pro Tem Dickinson question: "contractors would be expected to be up to date on at least up at least the 2018 code since we have a 2021 code in place. Why why wouldn't they be required to be up to date to the to the current code that we're implementing?"

>> Zukero response: "I think it's the testing standard that they have to meet. Um it's 28. It says 2018 or later... we could change that language potentially if this is an issue just to say the the most recent version of the code that the city has adopted."

>> Council Member Cooperman request: "I'd really like to see some kind of requirement for education or training around heat pump and heat pump hot water heater installations."

>> Mayor clarified: "I think the time for amendment will be at the public hearing."

## Notes
The meeting featured extensive public comment from environmental advocates and former Boulder Open Space planner criticizing the PROS long-range plan contract for lacking specific ecological deliverables, excluding open space management planning, and creating potential for future community conflict. Public hearing for Ordinance 1895 set for May 20, 2025.
