**Meeting Type:** Special City Council Meeting  
**Date:** January 13, 2026

[ROLL CALL] - Full council present

[MAYOR'S OPENING REMARKS]

**Context:**
- Comprehensive plan updated every ~10 years (last: 2013)
- Current draft represents ~2 years of work
- Process started with housing plan development
- "Astonishingly good" public engagement throughout
- In "home stretch" of process
- Mayor acknowledges not everyone will agree with every element but emphasizes shared care for city's future

**Tone:** Civil, thoughtful discussion expected; high regard for public input

[STAFF PRESENTATION - Jeff Hurt, Community Development]

**Purpose:** Introduce 95% draft ahead of broader community information sharing

**Request Level:** Clarification-level feedback, major omissions, policy questions (not line-by-line edits at this stage)

**What Comprehensive Plan IS:**
- Policy document (NOT regulatory)
- High-level, community-driven vision
- 20-year horizon
- Updated every 10 years
- Update to 2013 comp plan

**What it IS NOT:**
- Zoning
- Regulations
- Prescriptive budget document

**Process to Date:**
- 2+ years of work
- Over 2,000 residents engaged
- Consultation with all boards and commissions as subject matter experts
- Three community engagement phases
- Summer 2025: Council deep dive on early draft + area-by-area future land use framework review
- Current 95% draft reflects that summer direction

**Document Structure:**
- Vision and values
- Goals and strategies organized by theme
- Future land use framework (mapping + capacity analysis)
- Implementation chapter
- Detailed appendices

**Key Themes Emphasized:**
- Part of continuum of city efforts (not standalone)
- Focus on maintaining high level of services and quality of life
- Plans to begin immediately after adoption to continue progress

**FUTURE LAND USE FRAMEWORK (Detailed Discussion)**

**Purpose:** Planning tool showing where different types of development could occur over 20-year horizon

**Context from Summer 2025 Council Direction:**
- Deep area-by-area review conducted
- Council provided specific direction on land use designations
- Current draft reflects that direction

**Growth Estimates Discussion:**
- Framework projects theoretical residential capacity range
- NOT a growth target or cap (state law prohibits growth caps in comprehensive plans)
- Conservative planning tool for infrastructure and service needs
- Actual development expected to be significantly lower due to market realities, development constraints, public approval processes

**Housing Capacity vs. Housing Need:**
- Housing needs assessment: ~2,500 units (maintaining Louisville's current share of Boulder County population based on state projections)
- Capacity range in plan: Higher number representing theoretical maximum based on land use framework
- Difference explained: Capacity must account for infrastructure planning, market uncertainties, and provide flexibility
- Many properties designated for residential may stay commercial or redevelop at lower densities

**State Law Constraint:**
- Colorado law explicitly prohibits municipalities from imposing growth caps in comprehensive plans
- Plan must provide framework, not numerical limits

**Public Process Emphasis:**
- All major development projects continue to require public hearings before Planning Commission and City Council
- Comprehensive plan provides policy framework for evaluating those future proposals
- Not prescriptive—allows case-by-case evaluation balanced against multiple policy goals

[COUNCIL DISCUSSION]

**Key Questions/Topics:**
- Clarification on future land use framework methodology
- Discussion of housing capacity projections vs. needs
- Questions about implementation timeline and next steps
- Concerns about community understanding of capacity vs. targets

**Recurring Theme:** Balancing community desires for neighborhood preservation with housing affordability/accessibility and fiscal sustainability

[PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD]

Significant public input representing diverse perspectives on:
- Housing capacity numbers and growth concerns
- Neighborhood character preservation
- Affordable housing needs
- Infrastructure capacity
- School impacts
- Environmental sustainability

[COUNCIL CLOSING DISCUSSION]

Council members expressed general support for process and direction while noting ongoing refinement needed on specific elements before final adoption.

**Next Steps:**
1. Staff incorporates council/public feedback into final draft
2. Community information sharing sessions
3. Planning Commission review and recommendation
4. City Council final adoption

---

**Note:** This City Council discussion preceded the February 26, 2026 Planning Commission meeting where commissioners unanimously voted to recommend plan adoption (separate meeting in knowledge base).

**Overall Takeaway:**
Louisville's comprehensive plan update represents two years of community engagement and iterative refinement. The 95% draft before council reflects prior direction with key tension around housing capacity projections vs. community desires for growth limits. State law prohibits growth caps, forcing plan to use capacity ranges as planning tools rather than hard limits. Process moving toward final adoption with additional community input and Planning Commission review ahead.
