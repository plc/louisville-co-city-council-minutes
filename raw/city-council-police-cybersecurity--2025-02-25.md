# Louisville City Council Study Session: Police Operations & Cybersecurity
February 25, 2025

## Police Department Operational Overview

### 2024 Achievements

>> Chief Rafer: "We seek to continue to deliver what Division Chief Miller has coined high value services. To show that we're meeting the standards that are set forth in our mission, values, and vision, we were formally recognized as a professional organization by the Colorado Association of Chiefs of Police. Initially in 2018, we received accreditation through that organization and then in 2024, we were reaccredited meeting the same standards of professionalism that the CACP has set forth."

>> Chief Rafer: "Staffing has been a tremendous hurdle for a lot of public safety organizations, but through the hard work of our staff, we were able to hire total of 12 new police employees last year. That actually brought us to full staffing for the first time in many years. As of December 24, we were at full staffing in all of our departments."

>> Chief Rafer: "We implemented a new management system called Frontline. That helped take us from a paper-based system into a web-based system in which we're able to manage our training records. We're able to manage our professional and administrative files. We've also created a portal through the city website on our web page in which community members can relay commendations, compliments or relay any concerns that they may have through that portal. It goes directly into the system which helps for tracking and for accountability purposes."

>> Chief Rafer: "Last year we started an e-bike patrol program, purchased three e-bikes. We trained three officers and two open space rangers. They are now certified through law enforcement bicycle association. Patrolling tactics, how to ride safely and things of that nature."

>> Chief Rafer: "We developed a marketing and recruitment campaign to address our staffing challenges. We created three recruitment videos which were very well done to highlight our department, highlight our community and really try to draw interest into our organization."

>> Chief Rafer: "We received over $320,000 in federal and state grants. That funding helped to support the co-responder services, some in-service training, create some high visibility traffic and DUI enforcement opportunities throughout the year, as well as purchasing various items of equipment."

>> Chief Rafer: "We moved our patrol scheduling to a platoon model, which means that we are kind of split equally. Half of our department works Sunday through Wednesday. The other half is Wednesday through Saturday, which provides 24-hour coverage, but it also helps us to implement a common day on Wednesday, which we're now utilizing for training purposes. So half of our department trains on one Wednesday of the month, the second half trains on the second Wednesday of the month. It's 10 hours of training covering post requirements, the different types of curriculum and information that they need to maintain their certification as well as to enhance their skills and abilities."

>> Chief Rafer: "We conducted a total of 23 formal community presentations and attended an additional 39 community events throughout the year last year. That's a credit to Scott Moore and his outreach efforts but more importantly also just our focus on engaging with the community and making sure that we're present and establishing those relationships."

>> Chief Rafer: "We did archive and digitize some old microfiche records. That project has been completed. That was a tremendous undertaking by our administrative services manager, Jen Kenny, and her team. It was really taking an antiquated way of maintaining those records, moving it into a more contemporary way of doing that."

>> Chief Rafer: "We also created a guiding document for our agency that really spans the next three years, 2025 through 2028. It was meant to replace a previous document that had been in place that covered 2017 through 2021."

### 2025 Goals

>> Chief Rafer: "The department leadership team developed specific goals for 2025 for our agency. You'll find that they center around common themes of community engagement and partnerships, service delivery, as well as recruiting the best candidates and retaining our incredible staff. We're looking at innovative ways to accomplish all of our missions. The goals that we have are very specific and intentional. They align with our value statement, our mission, and our objectives."

>> Chief Rafer: "Prioritizing proactive policing, crime prevention, and increase visibility supporting community policing principles. So we're going to focus on traffic, pedestrian, and bicycle safety. We're going to use data and statistics to increase our overall contacts by 5% from the previous year. So it gives us an achievable point, a specific point that we're targeting in terms of increasing our enforcement, our visibility."

>> Chief Rafer: "We want to participate more in community events. So I mentioned the number of events that we attended in 2024. We're going to look to increase that by 5% in 2025."

>> Chief Rafer: "We've created an initiative called the Shield Initiative in which it really frames our community policing model. The intention of our department to engage with community and that was created by Division Chief Miller. We presented that to our staff this year to kick that off and it involves again a lot of the community engagement I mentioned here."

>> Chief Rafer: "In line with what Colorado and the nation have experienced over the last few years, within 2023 2024, those numbers actually improved in terms of the rate of auto theft was decreasing. But it still is an issue and it's still a crime that does occur within our community and surrounding areas. And so we're focusing on that specific type of crime."

>> Chief Rafer: "We want to deliver high value service. So we want to seek to achieve 90% satisfaction rating in our performance our overall performance as it relates to city community surveys. On the most recent survey we scored 87% overall. So we want to bump that number up to at least 90%. So that's our goal and that's our objective."

>> Chief Rafer: "Enhancing recruitment and retention practices. As I mentioned, as of December, we were fully staffed. We are now down one officer who looked for other opportunities in another state. And so we know that there's a constant ebb and flow with our staffing. And so we don't want to lose sight of that being an initiative that we have to remain on top of, remain engaged with prospective candidates who only promote our department as a place to work."

>> Chief Rafer: "We want to seek innovative ways to increase use of technology and data analysis to enhance our service delivery capability. Looking at technology, working with the IT department and other partners, just trying to see what can we do better with technology to help enhance our resources to help make us more effective and more efficient."

### Department Structure

>> Chief Rafer: "We have 35 total sworn certified officers. 28 are assigned to patrol. We have four assigned to detectives. We have one division chief, one deputy chief, and one police chief that makes up our command and executive staff. Five and a half full-time employees support staff position. We have an administrative services manager. We have three records staff and one and a half full-time employees that are our evidence custodians. We also have two non-certified uniform staff which consists of our code enforcement as well as our police support technician. We have our crime prevention specialist and public information officer. And then we have one senior administrative assistant to the chief. So that encompasses our department about 45 individuals work for the Louisville Police Department in various capacities."

### Operations vs. Services Division

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "I kind of liken a police department to a restaurant. In a restaurant, you have front of the house staff and then you have the back of the house staff. Front of the house is usually the hostess or hosts or maitre d' wait staff management for front of the restaurant and the back of the house you have the cooks the kitchen the kitchen manager and all those folks who do the work on the back end."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "In the police department operations would be uniform services primarily. So they are the ones who are the first ones to respond to calls for service, the first investigators to crimes, most public facing group that we have probably in the police department. And so that includes patrol and then code enforcement for us."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "The back of the house is the services division. Division chief Mike Miller, who is actually out at an incident we have going on right now, which is why he's not with us. He is walking the talk right now. The back of the house he's primarily responsible for all of the services. Investigations, property and evidence, records, those are all support services. Once operations creates the work on the front end or fields the work initially on the front end, our support services staff processes it on the back end to catalog intake and dispose of property and evidence. They process all of our police reports. They're the ones who collaborate with the district attorney's office municipal court public records requests all of those kinds of things."

### Detective Bureau

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "We have one detective sergeant who supervises two soon to be three detectives. They handle both persons and property crimes. Primarily they handle felonies, but they do occasionally help out with misdemeanors and they're also a resource to our patrol staff. If they need court orders for something or a search warrant or production of records warrant or something that patrol just doesn't necessarily field on a daily basis, our detectives are really our subject matter experts for courts, warrants, and things like that."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "They're assigned approximately 15 new cases every month. There's a steady stream of that because again this is part of the back end of the house. So as operations brings the cases in on the front end and detectives work them on the back end."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "They handle everything from motor vehicle theft, burglaries, and other property crimes. Sometimes attempted homicide case which we had last month when a guy decided to try to drown his girlfriend in her bathtub. So it's never a dull moment for detectives."

### Property & Evidence Unit

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "Property and evidence is where we have one and a half employees, one full-time evidence technician, Eric, and one part-time technician, Mike. When our officers seize evidence, seize property, either in an official investigation or if it's found property, or if someone turns property in that they've found or they want destroyed or something like that, they're the ones who take care of all of that, the intake, the cataloging, and then the disposal of all of that."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "In 2024, property and evidence took in 1,177 items, disposed of 137 items, and returned 252 items to owners. And that could be either things that have been returned by order of the court or found property or something along those lines. Altogether 1,566 items total."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "We know that that's the exact number of items we have. No runs, no drips, no errors because we conduct an annual audit of our property and evidence unit and Jen Kenny along with Mike and Erica and Scott Moore who helped with that process as well just concluded our annual audit and everything without one exception is accounted for, cataloged properly, stored exactly as it should be. So we're very proud of that."

### Records Unit

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "The records unit, they are the folks that you will meet if you come into the police department right behind the glass. They're the first ones there. They manage and process really all the other work that we produce. Police reports, public records request, case filings. They prepare all that for misdemeanor and felonies for municipal court district attorney's office and then handle all they're kind of the primary contact for the public right there at the front window at the police department."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "Just in 2024, our records staff facilitated 573 public records requests. That is an ever growing thing as technology advances. When the body camera law came into effect as you guys all know in 2017, now they are now responsible for body cam footage redaction processing and then fulfilling those requests as we get those as well. So that's a new piece that law enforcement really has never had until now. So their job grows."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "They processed 169 background check requests, almost $6,000 in records request payments for police reports and other records and 2,116 police reports that our staff are generating. So they too like property and evidence are very busy."

## Compensation Challenges

>> Chief Rafer: "We did conduct a staffing analysis actually prior just prior to my arrival. It was conducted the end of 22 and the beginning of 23. It showed that we were in terms of the level of service delivery and what we would like to achieve we were at less officers than what we would need. You know, I presented a potential growth strategy to try to increase the number of authorized officers so that we could continue to offer higher levels of service."

>> Chief Rafer: "There's a desire to expand opportunities. We want to be a part of the county drug task force. Currently, we don't have the staffing to be able to do that. So we're losing some of these opportunities because we don't have our core patrol group at a level where we're comfortable to start moving people to specialized areas."

>> Chief Rafer: "We are in a bidding war with other departments and other agencies across the region. We are our own worst enemies in that regard because other communities that are very nearby are now offering pay and compensation packages that are significantly better than ours."

>> Chief Rafer: "Deputy Chief Fischer has done a report and an analysis of compensation packages across the region. We are pretty far behind everybody else and so we are trying to sell quality of work life the quality of the community as our number one primary recruiting tool because if it's simply a dollar for dollar comparison we are significantly lacking. We're lacking towards the higher end like the last few steps of our pay scale."

>> Chief Rafer: "When you're looking prospective candidates looking at departments in our area, the pay range is less in Louisville than it is in other departments. And so that has become a recruiting challenge. We've had to kind of refocus our strategy to say, look, you may not be paid the highest, but you have a great supportive community. It's a very good working environment. Work pace is going to allow you to build relationships and you have a fantastic command staff. So that is our selling point."

*Council member asked about competitiveness compared to other jurisdictions*

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "Frankly speaking we are last in our region. We are the bottom eight department in this area. It's a survey of about 40 different agencies."

>> Deputy Chief Fischer: "We are competitive entry level in cadet. We are paying similar wages and we have good benefits. But where we run into the significant deviation is probably about step three or step four out of our six-step plan to the top end officers. Communities are paying significantly more. And so what that means is we'll have somebody for three to four years. Now they're like, well, I can go to this community and make significantly more and it's nothing against the department or the community, but they're like personally I can be paid more to do this similar job in some other community."

Since its implementation in April of last year we saw the number of calls in which they were able to respond with and intervene those consistently went up month after month. ## Cybersecurity Presentation

### Threat Landscape

>> IT Director: "Arctic Wolf is a partner of ours. We partner with an organization called Arctic Wolf to provide us with managed security operations center. If I were to have a 24/7 shop, because threat actors do not sleep and they usually infect you in the middle of the night, on the weekends, on holidays, all of those really inconvenient times. If I were to staff that and have that on site, that would be millions of dollars that we would be investing in to provide 24/7 365 support for constant monitoring of our environment. Arctic Wolf does this for us and they are a partner with us."

>> IT Director: "They have sensors within our environment. They are constantly monitoring our traffic. It's not just the sensors but it's actual human analysts as well. We met with them this morning and they provide us with a lot of data and information."

### Vulnerability Management

>> IT Director: "We have 4,310 vulnerabilities. Of those, we've mitigated 1,875 of them, so almost 2,000. We've accepted 81. We've confirmed false positives on 10 of them and we have 2,200 that are remaining. So there's a lot. This is us going in. This is we are evaluating, reviewing these and confirming like this is one at a time kind of stuff that we're constantly reviewing."

>> IT Director: "Today I found out that they're raising higher and faster than we are able to mitigate but we're doing the best job that we possibly can making sure that we're hitting the high and the critical vulnerabilities and risk for the organization."

*Council member asked what kinds of things are vulnerabilities*

>> IT Director: "Could be anything. Could be a patch that Microsoft has released. These numbers are very flexible over time. You can look at it day-to-day and change. But it could be a patch, could be a piece of software that's misconfigured, could be somebody installed a piece of software that's known vulnerable that we haven't gotten the report that that software is on the network yet to go and remove it. Arctic Wolf has identified it."

>> IT Director: "The remaining 2,200 items for the most part are small settings that Arctic Wolf would like us to set on individual machines. The problem is if you're going to set that setting you need to do it with like a system-wide review to make sure it's not going to impact production. So each of these take time to sit down understand exactly what that setting is. Arctic Wolf can only provide you so much information. They don't necessarily know what the impact's going to be to the organization of setting that setting but that is best practice to turn that one specific item on."

### Frameworks & Partnerships

>> IT Director: "What are we doing here in the city of Louisville to improve our cyber security posture? The frameworks that we follow are industry frameworks called NIST and the Center for Internet Security also has a framework and we kind of go back and forth between both of those evaluating the different frameworks and deciding what risk we're willing to take what risks we have to take or how we're going to mitigate risk and resolve issues. We follow these frameworks. These are national frameworks that other organizations follow."

>> IT Director: "We work with an organization and partner with an organization called Rule 4. They are a local Boulder County based organization that is phenomenal. They are cyber security experts. They have helped many of the organizations we were just talking about recover from their incidents and cleanse their environments so that they are good to go and they don't have a re-ransomware attack. Like if you don't find out what is causing the ransomware attack in your environment and you just go spin everything back up again you're just going to get hit again. So they help organizations do that."

>> IT Director: "They also help us on the front end so they help us create and implement and test our incident response plan. They have given us guidance on what others are doing, what the industries, what they're seeing. And they help us work through the NIST and CIS frameworks to decide how we're going to implement what we need to implement first. There's hundreds of different things if not thousands that we need to prioritize or do. So we have to decide where we put our attention."

>> IT Director: "We also partner with CIPA. CIPA is a Colorado state internet authority. They are essentially an organization that we can get grants through. We can get funding through. So we applied for grants. I just applied for two of them through them last week. And we received CrowdStrike through them. And are very lucky to have them as a partner."

>> IT Director: "MS-ISAC is also another really cool government offering that they kind of share information with us as things are brought out. Zero days are recognized, critical vulnerabilities are recognized, they send that information out to us and let us know what we can do to mitigate. So they're constantly kind of giving us and feeding us information."

>> IT Director: "And then we have Seagate as well, which are the other government IT agencies that are out there throughout the state of Colorado. We come together, we share information, we provide resources to each other, you name it, we're out there to try to help each other. So we're all kind of taking and giving from that."

### Recent Security Improvements

>> IT Director: "I think when Daniel and I came on board, actually the same day that chief came on board the chief of police, so it's been less than two years. I think one of the first things I did was go to hire a senior network engineer and make sure that we had a strong networking understanding on our team because when I got here, I was like, wow, we can't do cyber security well if we don't have someone who truly understands networking and is just a brain and a whiz at that."

>> IT Director: "Daniel came on board and both of us I think it was pretty clear that we both wanted to go in a very different direction than where we were for cyber security when I got here. I have been incredibly grateful to all of you for I think believing in us and some of the requests that we've had come through for funding and ensuring that we are able to invest in cyber security the way we want to."

>> IT Director: "We reimplemented multi-factor authentication. We did not like how it was the first time around and we reimplemented the tool."

>> IT Director: "We isolated and upgraded all the servers in our SCADA environment for water and wastewater. At the time there were some of them still connected to the domain which means if we were hacked here people had a direct access to our water plant system. So we separated that out and no longer have that issue and they have a whole different other set of security policies and practices that they have to go through but it is best practice."

>> IT Director: "We did do a .us.gov transition and if some of you have been here with emails for long enough you may have been a part of that but .gov is more secure."

### Email Security

>> IT Director: "About 20% of our emails are blocked or not coming through every month" due to advanced protection and quarantine systems.
