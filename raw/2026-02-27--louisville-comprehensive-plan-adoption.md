>> I will now open up the public hearing on a request regarding the city's comprehensive plan. The purpose of the hearing is to receive evidence regarding the application materials and provide a public forum for all interests of parties who wish to comment on this.

[Staff Presentation - Rob Zuko, Community Development Director]

>> Good evening, Chair Bryan, members of the commission. I'm Rob Zuko, community development director for the city. Staff is pleased to present the final adoption draft of the city's comprehensive plan update. This is coming to the planning commission after two years of work and extensive community involvement in the development of the plan. There was amazing community input and involvement that exceeded our expectations.

>> This plan will guide Lewisville's path for the next 10 years and will update many of the city's policies to align with the vision and core values established in the plan.

**Key Plan Elements:**
- Supports maintaining established neighborhoods and historic downtown/Oldtown character
- Promotes new development in select areas for vibrancy and economic development
- Much larger focus on inclusive and equitable community
- Increased focus on community resiliency and sustainability

[Planning Manager Hurt - Detailed Presentation]

**Housing Capacity Discussion:**
- Plan projects 4,000-5,000 unit residential capacity range over 10-20 years
- This is a theoretical maximum, actual development expected to be significantly lower
- 3,600 units are on vacant land (most likely to develop)
- Remainder on already-developed properties (unlikely to redevelop)
- Many designated residential areas are commercial and may stay commercial
- State law EXPLICITLY PROHIBITS growth caps in comprehensive plans

**2,500 Unit Housing Need vs. 4-5K Capacity:**
>> The 2,500 number in the housing needs assessment is very different than that 4 to 5,000 unit metric in terms of its purpose. The housing number is about need and the 4 to 5,000 is about both housing need but also infrastructure capacity planning.

>> If we based on our current percent of the overall Boulder County population, 20 years out, based on the state's projection of Boulder County's population, our increment would be 2500 units. [This is if Louisville maintains current percentage of county population]

>> If we did rerun the analysis and change the future land use framework so that it spit out something around 2500 units, that would significantly reduce our chances of actually achieving 2500 units because of all the factors we just talked about.

**State Law Restriction:**
>> There is a state law that explicitly restricts any city from having a growth cap of any kind in a comprehensive plan or any other regulatory document. We can't legally say we want to see 2500 units and stop there.

**Public Process Maintained:**
>> We expect to continue to have the system in place where basically any multifamily residential development and anything of any scale or size will continue to have a public process of approval in front of planning commission and city council.

[Public Comment Period]
