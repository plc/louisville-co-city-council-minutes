## Public Comments

### Via Appia Striping Safety Concerns
>> George Colbert (Lewisville resident) raised urgent safety concerns: "I've talked to you many times and raised my concerns about via Apia and the failed striping, whatever we're calling it, multiple times. I've started to feel like a broken record, so I've given you a break and I've quit complaining about it."

>> Ongoing danger: "I continue to be involved in near misses at the intersection of Tyler and Via. It happens almost daily. It was dangerous before the failed striping and now it's much worse. It has has got to be the top two or three most dangerous intersections in the entire city in my opinion."

>> Specific incidents: "When I'm driving, cars ride up on me because there's only one lane now. They try to push me down via AIA. And when they're not happy, they pass me on the left hand side in the yellow stripe lane. The worst part though is we brilliantly removed the right-hand turn lane."

>> Recent motorcycle incident: "Today I rode my brand new motorcycle all the way to Denver in the HOV lane all the way back. Not one issue until I got to Tyler and via AIA. Same thing. I go to make the proper and legal right-hand turn. The car behind me swoops in to the right and comes within two feet of me. I could see the kid's face clear as day."

>> Demand: "Via AIA needs to be fixed and it needs to be fixed right now."

## Ordinance 1892 - Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU) - Second Reading

>> Title: "an ordinance amending title 17 and title three of the Lewisville Municipal Code concerning the regulation of accessory dwelling units in the city of Lewisville."

### Staff Presentation - Jeff Hurt, Community Development

>> Current state: "LSEL does not currently allow ADUs. That does make us an outlier among peer cities. Most cities in our region have allowed them for some time now."

>> State law requirement: "the state did pass a law last year that requires all cities, including Lewisville, all cities of a certain size, including Lewisville, to allow ADUs as accessory to a single family unit... The deadline for compliance is coming soon. It's by the end of June."

>> Key restrictions: "these cities have to allow ADUs administratively, meaning you can't have like a discretionary process. You can't have like subjective standards that say, you know, must be consistent with neighborhood character, things like that. It has to be very black and white that they're allowed."

### ADU Ordinance Key Provisions

>> Where allowed: "essentially all areas that have an allowance for a single family unit would be allowed to have an ADU under this ordinance... that's by right with no discretionary review process. Um, the only exception for that is that if it's potentially a historic building where the ADU would go, there is the ability for the city to have historic preservation review at a public hearing."

>> Size limits: "the maximum ADU size under the proposal is for a detached ADU 800 square f feet... or an attached ADU which would be contiguous with the existing structure within it. Um 1,000 square feet would be the maximum under this proposal."

>> Incentive bonuses: "if the unit is landmark potentially landmarked deed restricted affordable housing or designed as an accessible unit meaning accessible to um someone with the disability um there is an automatic 200 square foot bump um to both sizes."

>> Height limits: "the maximum height for a detached ADU would basically mirror what you can already do for an for a detached accessory building. So, if you wanted to build a detached garage or detached shed, the maximum height is 20 ft, this would align with that... in Oldtown, our proposal is to allow a slightly taller detached ADU up to 25 ft."

>> Parking requirements: "the only place where we can require parking essentially is if you build an ADU and there's no on street parking on the subject block, which is very rare in Lewisville. and so and you don't have an excess space to access already on the lot."

>> Existing units grace period: "we did build in a grace period for existing units through the end of 2026 to be able to come into the city and get those units sort of legitimized as legal non-conforming units... we as a city would go in and do a life safety inspection of that unit. So we'd make sure there's adequate egress, carbon monoxide alarms are working, things of that nature."

>> Impact fee exemption: "the proposed ordinance... the first 500 square ft of an ADU is exempt from development impact fees that are assessed on all new residential units. Um, that would be treated the same as another type of addition to a house."

>> Tap fees: "tap fees are certainly a hot button issue and you'll hear more about that um, tonight I'm sure. Um, but we are not proposing any changes to our TAT fees at this time. We do recognize the significant barrier um, and the cost um, but we are not proposing any CAT fee changes as part of this ordinance, but that is slated for a future soon to be discussion with council."

>> Short-term rentals: "we prohibit short-term rentals in Lewisville. This does not change that. So, an ADU under this ordinance would not be allowed to have a short-term rental."

### Alternative Proposal from Architects Group

>> Staff acknowledged: "there were some very thoughtful comments in a letter by a group of architects sent in recently... so I've got a couple slides here just to go through the sort of the alternate proposal that we received."

>> Proposed modifications included:
- "allow a more of a lot coverage kind of bump for an ADU than what's currently in the ordinance... this proposal would allow you to go into that by 500 square feet"
- "allow ADUs with all attached single family units, so town homes, row homes, duplexes to also allow an ADU"
- "allow ADUs to be taller citywide. So up to 25 ft not just in Oldtown but everywhere in the city"
- "remove that the ADU can only be 49% of the size of the primary unit"
- "remove the so that grace period I mentioned for existing units to come into compliance... and just have it be sort of an indefinite um grace period for existing ADUs"
- "explicitly state that an ADU has to be on the same utility as the primary unit"
- Address tap fee cost barriers

>> Staff response on lot coverage: "from our perspective as planners, lot coverage is one of sort of the biggest most important metrics for neighborhood character, right? in terms of the scale, the massing of buildings. And so that's a policy discussion. Uh we didn't propose putting it in because it could have a you know potential impact on neighborhood character."
